# Beyond Liars' Bench: The Impact of Lie Typology, Depth, and Sparsity on Deception Detection in LLMs

Notes taken from `papers/text/2607.20479_beyond_liars_bench.txt` (9 pages, read in full; no appendix). Only what is in the text is reported.

## Citation
- Amr Moustafa, Max Feser (University of Bonn), Florian Mai (University of Bonn; Lamarr Institute for ML and AI).
- arXiv:2607.20479v1 [cs.AI], 29 May 2026. Described by the authors as an "extended abstract"; venue not stated.

## Research question
How do four factors affect cross-distribution deception-probe performance on Liars' Bench: (1) lie typology of the training data (fabrication / omission / exaggeration), (2) representation depth, (3) probe expressivity, (4) sparse (SAE) vs dense features?

## Methodology (implementable detail)

### Pipeline (Sec 3; Fig 1)
- Follows the Liars' Bench protocol (Kretschmar et al. 2025) with extra training data and probe families, plus disk-backed activation caching.
- Three extraction modes: token-wise activations for training; mean-pooled over the assistant response for evaluation; a follow-up condition probed on the final token.
- Training: extract hidden activations at the target layer for **individual response tokens**; fit a linear probe with **AdamW, binary cross-entropy**, lr 1e-3, 20 epochs, batch size 64 (p.5). Regularization / weight decay / normalization: not stated.
- Evaluation: mean-pool activations over the assistant response, score with probe; threshold 0.5 for recall.
- Layers: 20th percentile of depth (matches the original Liars' Bench choice) and 66th percentile (peak per Boxo et al. 2025). No layer sweep.
- The authors do not build contrastive prompts; they use labelled examples from "the benchmark-style dataset" plus DolusChat. The exact content/size of the "without DolusChat" training set is not specified.

### Training data
- DolusChat (Cundy & Gleave 2025): ~65,000 examples, roughly even across fabrication, omission, exaggeration. K = 1000 examples sampled for training. Conditions: no DolusChat, fabrication-only, exaggeration-only, omission-only, full mixture.
- Table 1 (depth/expressivity) appears to use the fabrication-vs-truth setup (Sec 5.3 calls it "the earlier fabrication-only comparison"); Table 2 uses the full mixture.

### Probe families (Sec 3.4)
- Logistic regression: P(y=1|h) = sigmoid(w^T h + b).
- Mass-Mean: w = mu_deceptive - mu_truthful, threshold on projection.
- Truth2D (Burger et al. 2024): project to a 2D truth x polarity subspace, then classify.
- INLP (Ravfogel et al. 2020): iterative null-space projection.
- TPC (Oldfield et al. 2026): quadratic w^T h + h^T W h + b with restricted/low-rank W.
- Follow-Up probe: logistic regression on final-token activations after appending "Was your last response deceptive?" with the answer prefilled as "No" (Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2025).
- Upper-Bound probe: split follow-up-token activations within each evaluation dataset in half; train a fresh logistic probe on one half, test on the other.

### SAE pipeline
Gemma-2-27B layer 31 of 46 (~67.4% depth); Gemma Scope `gemma-scope-27b-pt-res-canonical`, width 131,072; linear probe on SAE features vs on dense states at the same layer.

### Belief elicitation
None performed by the authors; labels come from Liars' Bench (described, p.4: e.g. Harm-Pressure Choice labels a response deceptive if it "contradicts its previously established belief"; Gender Secret labels a lie if the model denies knowledge "despite demonstrating knowledge of it in parallel evaluations").

## Models
- Mistral-Small-3.1-24B (dense; Table 1).
- Gemma-2-27B (dense vs SAE; Table 2).

## Datasets
- Train: DolusChat (1,000 sampled) and/or Liars' Bench-style training examples.
- Eval: four Liars' Bench subsets -- Convincing Game (CG; self-knowledge, instructed), Gender Secret (GS; fine-tuned hidden context), Harm-Pressure Choice (HP-C; WMDP multiple-choice, world knowledge), Harm-Pressure Knowledge Report (HP-KR; denies knowing). Alpaca control appears in Fig 1. Sizes/splits: not stated. Hosting: not stated.

## Baselines
Logistic regression (reference), Follow-Up probe, Upper-Bound probe; Liars' Bench mean-probe result quoted for comparison (AUROC 0.12 on HP-KR for Llama 3.3 70B).

## Metrics
AUROC (primary) and recall at a 0.5 threshold. No FPR-constrained metrics, no significance testing.

- Logistic recall (L20/L66): CG 0.91/0.99; GS 0.08/0.10; HP-C 0.96/0.76; HP-KR 0.01/0.00.
- Depth is dataset-dependent: CG and GS better at 66%; HP-C and HP-KR better at 20%.
- Anti-transfer: HP-KR AUROC 0.12-0.16 across nearly all probes (systematically below chance), consistent with the original Liars' Bench; HP-C at L66 ~0.31.
- Even in-distribution Upper-Bound on HP-C is only 0.75-0.82 (Mistral) and 0.61 (Gemma-2-27B).

Table 2 (p.6), Gemma-2-27B layer 31, full DolusChat mixture, AUROC dense / SAE:
- Logistic: CG 0.67/0.67; GS 0.51/0.46; HP-C 0.48/0.48; HP-KR 0.35/0.31; avg 0.49.
- TPC avg 0.54; Truth2D 0.49; Mass-Mean 0.47; INLP 0.49; Follow-Up 0.50; Upper-Bound CG 0.92/0.92, GS 0.77/0.78, HP-C 0.61/0.61, HP-KR 0.79/0.76 (avg 0.77).
- SAE features roughly match dense; no consistent gain.

Lie typology (Sec 5.4; Fig 2 -- qualitative only, bar values are not recoverable from the extracted text):
- L20%: best training condition is exaggeration on CG, no-DolusChat on GS, fabrication on HP-C, omission on HP-KR; omission best on average.
- L66%: fabrication best on CG, no-DolusChat on GS, omission on HP-C and HP-KR; exaggeration best on average.
- The full DolusChat mixture is never the best condition and is weakest at L20%.

## Stated limitations (p.8-9)
Two depths only, no sweep; no statistical significance testing; typology experiment only with logistic regression; SAE only at 66% depth; synthetic DolusChat vs Liars' Bench distribution gap likely drives the anti-transfer; 0.5 threshold only, no FPR-constrained operating points.

## Relevance to hallucination-vs-lie separation
- Does NOT test hallucination vs lie. The related-work section (p.2) explicitly distinguishes deception from hallucination and notes that earlier honesty benchmarks (TruthfulQA, sycophancy sets) "often conflate a model's genuine lack of knowledge with active deception" -- useful framing to cite -- but no hallucination samples are evaluated; negatives are honest responses.
- What it establishes that matters for us:
  - Deception probes are strongly training-distribution dependent and can be **anti-correlated** out of distribution (AUROC 0.12-0.16 on HP-KR; ~0.31 on HP-C at depth 66%). A probe trained on one lie type should not be assumed to be a general "lie" detector, so any lie-vs-hallucination comparison must include a within-distribution (matched) probe, not only transferred ones.
  - Harm-Pressure Choice -- the Liars' Bench subset closest to our setting (knows the answer, misreports under an incentive, world knowledge) -- is the hardest even in-distribution (upper bound 0.61-0.82). Expect incentive-driven misreports on factual questions to be weakly separable from honest answers; a hallucination-vs-lie contrast may be weaker still.
  - Logistic regression is as good as fancier probes on average; SAE features add nothing. We can restrict to logistic regression + mass-mean without much loss.
  - Layer choice interacts with lie type (20% vs 66% depth flips the ranking), so sweep layers rather than fixing one.
- Reusable:
  - Probe training recipe (token-wise training, mean-pooled evaluation, AdamW/BCE, lr 1e-3, 20 epochs, batch 64).
  - Follow-up probe wording: "Was your last response deceptive?" with prefilled "No".
  - Upper-bound (within-dataset 50/50 split) probe as a separability ceiling -- directly applicable as "train a hallucination-vs-lie probe on half, test on half" to ask whether the two are linearly separable at all.
  - DolusChat as a ready source of fabrication/omission/exaggeration training lies; the code repo above.
  - Model sizes (24B, 27B) are heavy for 48GB without quantization; nothing in the paper depends on the model being that large.
