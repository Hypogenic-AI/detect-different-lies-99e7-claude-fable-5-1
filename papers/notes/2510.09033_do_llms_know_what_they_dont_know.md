## Citation
Cheang, C. S., Chan, H. P., Zhang, W., Deng, Y. "Do LLMs Really Know What They Don't Know? Internal States Mainly Reflect Knowledge Recall Rather Than Truthfulness." Singapore Management University / DAMO Academy, Alibaba / SUTD. arXiv:2510.09033v3 [cs.CL], 17 Apr 2026. Venue not stated in the text.

## Research question
Do internal-state hallucination detectors track truthfulness, or only whether the model is recalling parametric knowledge about the subject? Tested by splitting hallucinations into those driven by subject associations and those not. (pp.1-2)

## Taxonomy (Sec 3.2 p.3)
- **FA (Factual Association):** correct output.
- **AH (Associated Hallucination):** wrong output produced by relying on input-triggered parametric associations with the subject (e.g. "Barack Obama was born in the city of Chicago").
- **UH (Unassociated Hallucination):** wrong output produced without reliance on parametric associations to the subject (e.g. unknown person "Brenda Johnston was born in the city of" -> "Portland").

## Methodology (implementable detail)

### Data (Sec 3.1, A.1)
- Wikidata triples (s, r, o); subjects are persons. Four relations with cloze templates (Table 3 p.13):
  - father (P22): "The name of the father of [subject] is"
  - mother (P25): "The name of the mother of [subject] is"
  - spouse (P26): "The name of the spouse of [subject] is"
  - date of birth (P569): "The birth date of [subject] is"
- Generation: greedy decoding (temperature 0), BF16 precision (p.14). The prompts are raw completions (base checkpoints, no chat template mentioned).

### Correctness labelling (A.3 p.14)
Two stages: spaCy NER extracts the target entity and compares to gold; otherwise Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct judges using the prompt in Fig. 11 (grades A CORRECT / B INCORRECT / C WRONG GOLD / D ERROR; full prompt on p.14).

### AH vs UH labelling by causal intervention (Sec 3.3 pp.3-4)
- Block attention from the subject tokens to the last token and measure Jensen–Shannon divergence between original and intervened next-token output distributions.
- Threshold = **mean JS divergence over all correct answers (FAs)**. Hallucination with JS above threshold = AH; otherwise UH.
- In the analysis section, attention knockout follows Geva et al. (2023) with a window of 5 layers around layer ℓ (p.5). Which layers/window are used for the *labelling* intervention is not spelled out beyond "blocking attention from the subject tokens"; see the code repo.

### Mechanistic analyses (Sec 4)
- Interventions on subject-token states, subject->last attention, and last-token states per layer (Fig. 2): FAs and AHs have similar causal profiles; UHs much weaker.
- Subject representation norms: AH/FA ratio ≈ 1 across layers; UH/FA drops to ≈ 0.95 at layers 10–15 in LLaMA-3-8B (p.5).
- Subject popularity (monthly Wikipedia page views; three bins): low bin FA 5% / AH 1% / UH 94%; mid FA 27% / AH 7% / UH 66%; high FA 52% / AH 14% / UH 34% (Fig. 4 p.6, LLaMA).
- Last-token cosine similarity within category: ≈ 0.9 early for all; by layer 25, FAs and AHs ≈ 0.2, UHs ≈ 0.5 (p.7). (The y-axis of Fig. 6 runs 0.3–0.9, so the "≈0.2" in the prose is inconsistent with the figure axis; reported as printed.)
- Output entropy: FAs/AHs low, UHs high (Fig. 8, no numbers).
- Cluster separability at last token (Table 4 p.17): UH vs FA silhouette 0.397 (LLaMA) / 0.327 (Mistral), DBI 0.932 / 1.134; AH vs FA silhouette 0.002 / −0.001, DBI 14.22 / 25.25.

### Detectors evaluated (Sec 5 pp.7-8)
"We extract and normalize internal features and then train a probe." Probe type and regularisation are not specified in the text (related work calls the classifier "often a linear probe").
- **Subject representations:** last subject token hidden state from three consecutive layers (Gottesman & Geva 2024). Which three layers is not stated.
- **Attention flow:** attention weights from the last token to subject tokens across all layers (Yüksekgönül et al. 2024).
- **Last-token representation:** final token hidden state from the last layer (Orgad et al. 2025).
- Scalar baselines: answer token probability; subject popularity.
- Appendix: semantic consistency, 200 instances per category, 5 samples per prompt at temperature 0.7 (E.2).

### Evaluation settings
- AH Only: 1,000 FA + 1,000 AH train; 200 each test.
- UH Only: 1,000 FA + 1,000 UH train; 200 each test.
- Full: 1,000 FA + 1,000 mixed hallucinations train; 200 each test.
- Five random seeds; mean AUROC ± sd.

### Refusal tuning (Sec 6, A.2)
QLoRA via Unsloth, rank 8, α 8, all attention and MLP modules, 1 epoch; 1,000 hallucination samples paired with 10 refusal templates + 1,000 FAs kept; test on 200 each of FA/UH/AH.

## Relevance to hallucination-vs-lie separation
- **No lies in this paper.** There is no pressure or incentive condition; every false output is an unpressured error. It says nothing directly about lie detectors.
- **What it does establish:** hallucination detectors (hidden-state probes, token probability, popularity, self-consistency) mostly detect "the model is not recalling stored knowledge about this subject" (UH), not falsehood. When a false output is produced via the same recall pathway as a true one (AH), detectors are near chance (0.48–0.69).
- **Implication for our question (inference, not a claim in the paper):** a lie told about a fact the model knows is produced with the subject knowledge present and recalled. If hallucination detectors key on recall rather than truth, a knows-but-misreports output may look like FA/AH to them and go undetected, while a doesn't-know output looks like UH. This predicts hallucination detectors should separate "doesn't know" falsehoods from lies for a reason unrelated to honesty, and it is a concrete hypothesis our experiment can test.
- **Warning for our "doesn't know" bucket:** it is heterogeneous. Confidently wrong beliefs (AH-like, consistent across samples, low entropy) behave differently from no-knowledge guesses (UH-like, high entropy). Under a MASK-style belief check, AH-like errors will show up as a consistent but wrong belief and UH-like errors as "no belief"; these should be reported separately.
- **Reusable:** the four Wikidata relation templates; the popularity split as a cheap way to get known vs unknown entities; the three probe feature sets (last subject token, attention-to-subject, last token/last layer); answer-token probability and 5-sample consistency as non-probe hallucination detectors; the 1,000+1,000 train / 200+200 test, 5-seed protocol; the JS-divergence attention-knockout label. Models are 7–8B, so everything fits on a 48 GB GPU.
